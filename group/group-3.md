# 🐋 Group 3: Marine Acoustic Monitoring

[← Back to overview](../README.md)

**About the study:** The study used hydrophone recordings from the North Sea to monitor whale communication alongside ship noise. It found that when ship noise was high, whales called less often and changed the pitch and loudness of their calls, indicating that shipping noise interferes with their ability to communicate.

### About our data
**Marine Acoustic Monitoring** – hydrophone recordings from the North Sea monitoring whale communication and ship noise.

This dataset is intended for:
- 🧪 Bioacoustics research – studying whale communication and behavior
- 🎶 Signal processing – exploring sound patterns and spectrograms
- 🤖 Machine learning projects – classification, clustering, and audio analysis
- 📚 Education and outreach – demonstrating marine mammal vocalizations

Following are the columns of this dataset: Audio Sample, Whale Species, Water Body, Time, Duration, Sampling Frequency, Maximum Amplitude, Maximum Frequency, Organisation.

It can be accessed at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07421-0

### Making our data FAIR

| Principle | What we will do |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Findable** (persistent identifiers, rich metadata, indexed in a searchable resource) | The protocol is open, free, and universally implementable standard web protocols (e.g.: HTTPS): Ocean Networks Canada https://data.oceannetworks.ca/SearchHydrophoneData |
| **Accessible** (retrievable via open protocols, metadata available even if data is restricted) | Australian Ocean Data Network (Australian marine and climate science data), the protocol is open, free, and universally implementable standard web protocols (e.g.: HTTPS): Ocean Networks Canada https://data.oceannetworks.ca/SearchHydrophoneData |
| **Interoperable** (standard formats, shared vocabularies) | The data and its description are saved in common, standard file formats that computers can read automatically, and all descriptions are written in English (FLAC for voices, CSV for tables and description as JSON). We use official, shared lists of terms instead of our own words: official scientific names and ID numbers for whale species from WoRMS (World Register of Marine Species), measurement and instrument terms from the NERC Vocabulary Server (NVS) NERC (Natural Environment Research Council) is the UK’s main funder of environmental research. Its Vocabulary Server (NVS), run by the British Oceanographic Data Centre, provides standard terms for marine data such as measurements, instruments and units, place names such as the North Sea from Marine Regions, and standard terms and units for underwater sound from ISO 18405. |
| **Reusable** (clear licence, provenance, community standards) | We include comprehensive metadata like hydrophone GPS coordinates, deployment depth, sampling rate, frequency range and target species (ship noise or whale noise or what kind of whale) |
